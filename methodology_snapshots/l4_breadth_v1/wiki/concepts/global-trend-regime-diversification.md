# Global Trend Regime Diversification

## Definition

Global trend regime diversification evaluates whether international markets provide diversification after accounting for their trend states and regime correlations, not only their country labels or return correlations.

[[wiki/sources/trend-and-stochastic-movements-in-international-financial-markets|Trend And Stochastic Movements In International Financial Markets]] argues that S&P 500, DAX, and CAC 40 can move in synchronized regimes, while Nikkei 225 may offer more distinct behavior in the reported sample.

## Why It Matters

A portfolio can hold several international indices and still be concentrated if they all enter downtrends together. For trend-following systems, diversification should therefore consider:

- Return correlation.
- Trend-state correlation.
- Whether markets are in strong uptrend, weak uptrend, neutral, or downtrend.
- Whether the strategy is long/short, long/cash, or always invested.

## Evidence From Source

The source reports high daily-return correlations among S&P 500, DAX, and CAC 40, including S&P 500-DAX `0.85` and DAX-CAC `0.88`. It reports lower correlations for Nikkei 225 against several other markets.

The source's conclusion is that true diversification requires understanding how regimes correlate, not merely holding several countries.
